Open Photogrammetry Lab

Measure the world from photographs

An open course in digital photogrammetry. Every chapter pairs a short explanation with something you can move, rotate or measure, and the labs use the same browser software from the first fiducial to the finished orthophoto.

How the course is organised

The course follows the path that a photograph takes on its way to becoming a map. Each step answers one question about the geometry, and each answer becomes a tool you use in the next step.

  • Concepts · 1How a photograph measuresCentral projection, image scale and why tall objects lean outward. Interactive 3D camera.
  • Concepts · 2Interior orientationFrom scanner pixels to camera millimetres. Lab: measure the fiducials of a real aerial photo.
  • Concepts · 3The test flight in 3DExterior orientation, stereo overlap and the base-to-height ratio, on the real terrain.
  • Concepts · 4–5Relative and absolute orientationBuild a stereo model, then fit it to ground control.
  • Labs · L1–L3Single model, aerial triangulation, special casesComplete projects on the course data sets, step by step.
  • Products · 6–7Terrain models and orthophotosImage matching, DEM editing and rectification.

What you need

A current browser on a laptop or desktop. A mouse makes measuring easier, though a trackpad works. Nothing is installed and nothing is uploaded: the software runs on your computer, inside the page.

The labs start with a sample data set, a pair of black-and-white aerial photographs (numbers 157 and 158) taken with a 153 mm survey camera and scanned at about 85 µm. The set includes ground control points, a terrain model and reference results, so you can check every step against a known answer. Once you are comfortable, the same tools accept your own scans and camera files.

Conventions used throughout

Ground
X east, Y north, Z up, in metres.
Camera
x, y in millimetres on the image plane, origin at the principal point, y toward the top of the photo.
Pixels
col to the right, row upward, measured from the bottom-left corner of the scan.
Angles
ω, φ, κ, rotations about the X, Y and Z axes.
